# Three-minute judge presentation — Team Vydex (three members)

## 0:00–0:25 — Mission designer

“Vydex turns a spacecraft manifest into a mission you can fly. Every component has a mounting location, an information guide and consequences. Earth orbit, Moon, Mars and a clearly fictional asteroid are playable; the gas-planet destinations are labeled previews.”

Select **Mars 3-minute demo**. Skip the tutorial for the timed presentation; point out that Replay Tutorial is available without resetting progress. The demo uses the same engine and prepared, valid initial conditions.

## 0:25–0:50 — Mission designer

Select the spectrometer, open its **ⓘ** panel and show mass, cost, active power, mounting location and what happens if it is omitted. Close it: the same spacecraft remains. Install it: launch mass rises by 8 kg and mission cost by 6 credits. Remove it: “Our imaging objective does not need this instrument.” Rotate or briefly use Exploded view. Run preflight and authorize launch.

## 0:50–1:15 — Simulation engineer

Complete the three checklist actions. Choose **Follow** and reduce camera distance to see the payload inside its fairing through Cutaway inspection if desired. Return to the normal presentation and watch the scripted booster/fairing release, payload, Earth parking orbit and departure.

“This is explicitly a provider cinematic, not fake calculated Earth-ascent telemetry. The payload is the spacecraft we assembled.”

During transfer choose Hibernate and execute the required correction. “The two power alternatives are genuinely calculated from the same starting charge. The burn consumes cruise propellant only. Mars uses a Kepler-timed idealized Hohmann model; other missions use distinct routes and clearly labeled assumptions.”

## 1:15–2:05 — Simulation engineer

Begin final descent and click Take control. Leave assist on; use a short lateral input and release. “Entry is compressed. These local instruments use actual altitude, velocity, mass, thrust, fuel, tilt and simplified drag.”

Show contact speed and used fuel, then deploy the rover. “The Moon instead uses 1.62 m/s² and no drag. We do not reuse Mars physics there.”

## 2:05–2:35 — Exploration and experience designer

Drive toward the nearby Sentinel using WASD; Space brakes. Alternatively, enable Earth-command mode and queue its destination. Collect the observation within 6 m.

“Hardware must be aboard, in range and powered. The timed camera load is accounted for. This observation result is fictional and is separated from genuine source facts.”

## 2:35–3:00 — Exploration and experience designer

Transmit. The acknowledgment completes the mission and opens the debrief. Point to science, remaining battery, consumed fuel, mission credits and the explicit score rules.

“Our signature is explaining what to change: fit the missing mount, remove optional mass, preserve energy, or brake earlier. The Earth mission adds controlled satellite deployment; the asteroid mission adds real fuel-consuming relative motion. Design deliberately, fly your systems, and return the evidence.”

On a slower GPU select Low graphics. Menus and explanation steps pause relevant simulation. Do not claim unmeasured FPS, live NASA telemetry, actual asteroid trajectory integration or formal challenge compliance.
